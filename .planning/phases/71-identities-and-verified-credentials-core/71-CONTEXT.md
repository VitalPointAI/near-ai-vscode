## Phase Boundary

Verified credentials become the only source of authorization attributes. Data decides who may open it from its own label. Every place that releases a key or serves data checks the same pure function, from evidence cited by hash, and checks freshness at the moment of action. Keycloak only authenticates.

In this phase (rows B and E of the research):
- **The research step:** JCS conformance (71-01, the tracer), `did:webvh` hosting and caching, status list sizing and lifetimes, and the FIPS posture for Ed25519 with `did:webvh` (O8) with the fallback ready (71-02).
- **Our narrow VC 2.0 profile** (`eddsa-jcs-2022`, second suite `ecdsa-jcs-2019`) and every credential type of the research's replacement table, plus `ClassificationAuthorityCredential` (71-03).
- **DIDs:** `did:key` for devices from Phase 69 keys, `did:webvh` for people and your organization's root (71-04 pure; 71-10 in use).
- **Bitstring Status List**, signed by the HQ status-list key in OpenBao Transit, each signature authorized only by an officer's step-up (O4) (71-05 pure; 71-12 the key and the lists in use).
- **Trusted issuers in the signed policy** (`credentials@1`, `agreements@1`) and the issuer authority chain (71-06).
- **The credential checker** used by the fold, the hub and the wrap preparers: evidence cited by hash, `mayRelease`, freshness at the enforcement points, maximum offline age per level (O6) (71-07), with the wallet and the gate's clearance (71-08).
- **Issuance from officers' own passkey sessions** (O4, O7) (71-12).
- **The hub** serves logs and lists and is an enforcement point (71-09); **the signed trust export** carries them to edges (71-11); **the session** is an enforcement point (71-13).

Not in this phase:
- The cell wrap v2 (P-256 + ML-KEM-768). It is Phase 33.2 (D-02).
- Agent delegation, delegation credentials and the chain check of DLGT-02 to DLGT-08. They are Phase 71.1 (D-01).
- The 33.1 entry shapes that cite evidence, the posting and observer call sites, and 47-19 to 47-21 and 47-14 as amended. Other planners own them; this phase gives them the API in PLAN-IDS.md.
- Anchoring roots on NEAR (L4), federation transport (L5), SD-JWT VC and BBS, foreign-issued credentials (O10: later, through L5 trust lists), Keycloak OID4VCI (research section 7: not proposed).

### The profile and the credentials

- **D-05 (profile, VCRD-02):** VC 2.0 JSON with a fixed `@context` list: `https://www.w3.org/ns/credentials/v2` then our own context (owner item 3). Any other context is refused and no JSON-LD runs. Proof: `DataIntegrityProof` with `eddsa-jcs-2022` (Ed25519 Multikey, header `0xed01`), or the second suite `ecdsa-jcs-2019` (P-256 Multikey, header `0x1200`) per O8. Every proof names its suite. Integers only, no floats. `validFrom` and `validUntil` are required on every credential (nothing lasts forever). `credentialStatus` is required (one `BitstringStatusListEntry` for `revocation` and one for `suspension`) except on a status list credential itself. `credentialHash` is SHA-256 of the JCS bytes of the whole credential, proof included. Canonical JSON is the existing `src/domain/team/canonical.ts` once 71-01 proves it equals RFC 8785 for every input the profile allows. Signing goes through one `CredentialSigner { verificationMethod, suite, sign(bytes): Promise<Uint8Array> }`, so a device key, the hosted person key (Transit, asynchronous) and the offline root use the same path.
- **D-06 (credential types, O7):** The types and claims are the table in PLAN-IDS.md: ten person-attribute types, five authority types (`IssuerRoleCredential`, `SecurityContactCredential`, `ReleaseAuthorityCredential`, `ClassificationAuthorityCredential { scale, upTo, scope }`, `RoleCredential`), `DeviceBindingCredential`, and the W3C `BitstringStatusListCredential`. `DelegationCredential` is reserved for 71.1. Thresholds (VCRD-05): someone cleared to Secret holds one `ClearanceCredential` per level up to Secret; lowering a clearance revokes every higher threshold. `ClassificationAuthorityCredential` with `scope: own-items` is the originator's authority to lower their own items; its consumers (Phases 70.1 and 74.3 in the AI-classification research) are not in this phase. Type names are permanent once a credential is issued; claims are rated costly, not one-way, because entries cite credentials by hash and a credential can be re-issued under a new context version.
- **D-07 (agents and devices never issue, coordinator 2026-11-24):** A structural rule in the checker, not a promise. An issuer is your organization's root DID or a person `did:webvh` whose signing key is a Transit person key, which signs only after a passkey step-up that OpenBao itself enforces (role `sterling-workbench-stepup`, 47-07). `credentials@1` and every role credential may name only person `did:webvh` subjects. The checker refuses a credential whose issuer is a `did:key`, or whose issuer key is the subject of a `DeviceBindingCredential` or a delegation, whatever the policy says, and refuses a role credential naming such a subject. A property test over random chains (71-06) and a refusal test per path (71-06, 71-07). The root issues only role credentials, offline (D-13). Under the fallback (D-03, owner item 2) the only `did:key` that may issue is an officer's Transit person key that the signed policy names as a person key. **Owner confirmed 2026-11-24 ("defaults", owner item 2).**
- **D-08 (solo installs, owner item 1):** VCRD-07's sentence "a person with no organization issues their own credentials from their device DID" conflicts with D-07. Default: with no organization policy in force, the gate keeps using the person's own declared clearances on their own computer (Phase 70, the personal layer), shown as "declared, not verified". They are never credentials, never cited as evidence and never satisfy a check in a shared project, a hub, a wrap or your organization's policy. So a fresh install keeps working, nothing in it changes, and no device key ever issues. **Owner confirmed 2026-11-24 ("defaults", owner item 1); VCRD-07's text is rewritten to match.**

### The checker

- **D-09 (evidence by hash, research 3.3):** An entry that relies on a person's attribute cites it: `evidence` (credential hashes) and `status` (`{ list, hash, validFrom }` for each status list checked). The credentials travel as sealed blobs in the right cell. `checkEvidence` checks only the cited data, at the entry's signed time, under the policy the project's `project.mode` cites, so every replica gives the same answer whatever list it holds. The field names are proposed in PLAN-IDS.md; 33.1-01's checkpoint settles them in the entry shapes.
- **D-10 (`mayRelease`, research 3.4):** One pure function on `dominance.ts` decides every wrap, every release and every serve: level on the scale (or an approved mapping), every compartment, releasability for every holder of a marked cell (O5; empty marking means unrestricted; `RELEASABLE-TO-NONE` never matches), an `ExportEligibilityCredential` for each `CGP`, `ITAR` or `EAR` compartment, and the credentials required by sensitivity from the policy. Observers get the full dominance too, which closes the gap the research found in 33.1-19, 33.1-21 and 33.1-23. Handling stays the gate's routing. `eligibleForPosition` checks a position's `requiredClearance` and `eligibleServes`.
- **D-11 (freshness and offline age, O6):** `checkFreshness` is called only by enforcement points: the hub on append and serve, the wrap preparer, and the session. Defaults (examples the security officer sets in `credentials@1`): status time to live 15 minutes at a connected HQ; maximum offline age at an edge 7 days for Protected A, 72 hours for Protected B, 24 hours for any classified level. The hub refuses an append citing a list older than its newest list by more than the grace (default: the time to live). Clocks go through one helper, `credentialClock`: the entry's signed time plus the hub's witness time; offline, the last signed bundle's time and a monotonic counter (research flag 8; Phase 74 tightens it).
- **D-12 (one source of truth in the policy, DCAC-01):** `credentials@1` pins your organization's root DID and SCID, and names for each credential type the role credential an issuer must present and the ceiling of what that role may issue. It also holds the status lists, the time to live, the maximum offline age per level, the suites and DID methods allowed, and the credentials required by sensitivity (48.1 D-25's table), which a newer policy may only tighten. `agreements@1` holds organization-to-organization agreements (`{ id, partner, tokens, levelCeiling, compartments, until }`), the replacement for the `agreementRef` string. When `credentials@1` is present, the reserved v1 slots `statusLists` and `maxOfflineAge` must be empty (refused otherwise), so Phase 70's OFFL-03 slot is filled in one place. Who is an issuer is a role credential (revocable); what a role may issue is the policy. `people.signers` and the personal `people.clearances` grant nothing once `credentials@1` is in force (D-08 covers the solo case).

### Identities, issuance and the enforcement points

- **D-13 (DIDs):** Device and agent keys are `did:key` from the Phase 69 Ed25519 key with no key change. A P-256 `did:key` exists only for the strict-FIPS fallback (O8). People's `did:webvh` is created by the person's own backend at first sign-in, signed by their Transit person key after the sign-in step-up, and hosted by the sync hub under the site's ingress; HQ's hub is the witness. Whether people use pre-rotation is 71-02's verdict (default off; recovery is re-enrolment and a deactivation entry). Your organization's root `did:webvh` is made offline in a key ceremony with `scripts/org-issuer.mjs` (new commands `create-did`, `rotate-did`, `issue-role`), in its own sealed key file, separate from the Phase 41 policy key, with pre-rotation on: `create-did` makes the next key file at the same time and puts its hash in `nextKeyHashes`.
- **D-14 (status lists, research 3.2):** Status lists are signed by one HQ status-list key `status-list-<site>` in OpenBao Transit (ed25519, non-exportable); each signature is authorized only by an officer's step-up (O4). `credentials@1.statusLists` names, for each list, the public key that must sign it, and every verifier (the fold's `checkEvidence`, the hub, the wallet, the edge import) checks lists against that policy key only, never through the person-issuer rule. OpenBao lets it sign only for a token from a new jwt role `sterling-issuer-stepup`: bound to the step-up `acr` like `sterling-workbench-stepup`, and bound to the list of officer subjects (`sub`) that the site file's `credentialIssuers` names at the key ceremony. That is key custody by identity, not by a token attribute, and OpenBao itself enforces the step-up (as 46-07 requires). The officer's backend first checks the officer's role credential (authority). Both must allow; the ordinary session token never holds the `credential-issuers` policy. A revocation bit never clears. A suspension bit clears only by an issuer of that type, after a step-up. Lists have 131,072 entries (16 KB raw), random indexes and gzip; the hub stores them with a compare-and-swap on the list's version, so two officers never overwrite each other. 71-02 confirms the sizes and lifetimes.
- **D-15 (issuance, O4, O7):** In the officer's own workbench session, from a native "Issue credential" card. The backend checks the officer's role credential with `issuerAuthority`, then signs with the person key through `KeyService.sign` after a fresh step-up. The credential goes to the subject's wallet inbox in OpenBao (`kv/users/<entity>/credentials-inbox/<credential hash>`, which the `credential-issuers` policy may create and never read or update) or, for a single computer, to a `.near-vc` file (named so it is never confused with `~/.near-credentials`, which holds NEAR account keys). The subject's own backend moves inbox items into their wallet at session start, after verifying each. A person's wallet is SecretStorage under the key `near-ai.credentials`: OpenBao KV under the person's own prefix in a hosted session (46-07's `secretPath`) and the keyring on a single computer. Only the person's own backend reads it. The subject DID to entity mapping is a site directory `kv/site/people/<entity> = { did }`, written by each person's own backend through a policy templated on their own entity (so nobody can claim another person's DID) and readable by the `credential-issuers` policy only.
- **D-16 (the hub, research 4):** The hub serves `did:webvh` logs and status lists to members of the site, verifies every log append and list update it stores, and is an enforcement point: it checks cited evidence and freshness on every append that carries `evidence`, and refuses to serve a stream to a holder whose bit is now set. When a newer list sets a bit, the hub sends an event to the lead's clients, and a lead's client stages `cell.rekey` with the existing reason `clearance-lowered` (it now also means "a credential was revoked or expired"; no new value in `CELL_REKEY_REASONS`). The hub never signs a roster entry.
- **D-17 (the trust export, research 4.2, 47-14):** Its own file `.near-trust-export`, beside 47-14's signed user export and never inside `deploy/keycloak/users.mjs`. It is a bundle of objects that each verify on their own: log entries, signed status lists and the signed policy layer that holds `credentials@1`. An edge imports only what verifies and is newer than what it has, and offline age counts from each list's own `validFrom`. No new signing key.
- **D-18 (the session, research 4.1 and 4.3):** In a hosted session the backend checks `mayRelease` and freshness before it opens a cell's epoch key into the session, so ending the session or disabling the OpenBao entity cuts even granted epochs. The session's `active` state gains a credential sub-state: `fresh`, `stale` (past the time to live; protected actions are refused with a plain reason and Retry) and `unavailable`. A test proves the session backend and the hub read only `sub`, `acr`, `auth_time` and expiry from a token: extra claims or groups change no decision.
- **D-19 (the gate, VCRD-07):** When your organization's policy with `credentials@1` is in force, the gate's clearances come only from `clearanceFromCredentials` over the person's verified wallet; otherwise D-08 applies. O11: legacy `clearance` fields on Phase 31 member entries are declarations; `declaredClearanceCounts` lets them count only for unmarked and lowest-level cells once a project requires credentials.

- **D-22 (revocation requests, coordinator 2026-11-24):** When Keycloak disables or removes a person (47-31) or a guest's access ends early (47-20), the job writes one `sterling-revocation-request/1` record, once (`cas: 0`), at `kv/data/site/revocation-requests/<entity>-<reason>`: the entity name, the Keycloak id, the reason, the time seen and the job name; no secrets. One format for both. No script revokes anything: 71-12's card resolves the person through the OpenBao entity and the people directory, shows each officer only the credentials they issued to that person (from an issued register `kv/site/issued/<entity>/<credential hash>` written at issuance, with no credential body), and the officer revokes or suspends in their own session after a step-up. The request is deleted by the issuer step-up role, through a delete granted only on that path, once every credential issued to that person is revoked or suspended. A guest whose access ends on its date needs no request: their credentials' `validUntil` equals the end date.
- **D-24 (coordinator, 2026-11-24, reported to the owner; 71-10's checkpoint): a person's DID log reaches the hub through their own authenticated hub session.** The person's backend makes the did:webvh at first sign-in (signed with their Transit person key after the step-up), keeps the log under their own KV prefix and creates `kv/site/people/<own entity>`, and posts the log at the person's first hub session, through that session, once (`publishToHub`). Rejected: a hub route that takes a first log without a session (it would widen G426's first-come risk into an unauthenticated surface), and officers publishing people's logs (logs stay person-controlled). Until Phase 33's hosted session keys (33-21 to 33-23) are on `main` there is no such session, so the post is built and tested against a fake hub; gap G427.

- **D-25 (owner, 2026-11-25, option (a); closes G466): a person claims their own computer's status list places.** Binding one of their own computers (71-10) claims a revocation and a suspension place for its DeviceBindingCredential in the person's own session, through a narrow create-only permission in the session policy `sterling-workbench`:
  - **Their own slots:** `kv/site/device-slots/<own entity>/slot-1` to `slot-5`, create and read only, templated on the person's own entity. At most 5 computers are bound at once, and nobody can claim another person's slot or change a claimed one.
  - **The places:** `kv/site/status-index/device-<purpose>-<list>/<index>`, create only, in the top quarter of each list (from index 98,304). Officers' credentials draw below it, so the two never share a bit.
  - **Signing lists:** the person never signs a status list; that stays with the officers (D-14).
  - **A lost computer:** the person files a `computer-lost` request in the revocation inbox, under their own entity name only (`kv/site/revocation-requests/<own entity name>-computer-lost-<slot>`, create only). An officer revokes that binding after a step-up, then removes the slot, which frees it for another computer. When a person's access ends (D-22), their computers are listed and revoked with their credentials.
  - **Retrying:** a binding that stopped at the step-up uses the same slot again.
  - **Residual risk:** a binding signed outside the workbench path is not counted or revocable through the slots (G467).

- **D-26 (coordinator, 2026-11-25, G467): verifiers count a computer binding only from the person's own DID log.** A DeviceBindingCredential counts only when both its status places are 98,304 or higher, and the person's DID document in force at the time of the check lists that computer with exactly those places. The listing is one service entry `{ id: <did>#computer-<slot>, type: SterlingComputerSlot, serviceEndpoint: urn:sterling:computer:<revocation>:<suspension>:<did:key> }`, in one of at most five slots (`src/domain/credentials/computerSlots.ts`).
  - **Who checks:** `checkEvidence`, so the hub's append check, the wallet, the command-line check and 71-13's session all apply it. A known binding that is not cited only makes a key the member's under the same rule.
  - **Who writes the listing:** the person's backend, in their own log, signed with their person key after the step-up, before it signs the binding.
  - **No shared places:** the HQ hub refuses a log that lists more than five computers or uses a place another person's document lists.
  - **Rejected: a slot list signed by the status-list key.** An officer would have to step up for every new computer, and a computer would not count until the next list was signed. The DID log is already held and verified by every verifier, hosted by the hub and carried by the trust export, and every slot ever used stays visible in it.
  - **Rate limit:** the OpenBao rate limit quota `computer-places` allows 12 claims an hour per session and audits violations. The alert `ComputerPlaceClaimsLimited` reads OpenBao's own violation counter.
