## Phase Boundary

Your organization can send objects to allies and receive them at the release boundary, with labels a NATO system can read, bound to their content by a signature, and nothing leaves without the same credential check as any release. This part needs no restricted text. Our cells stay the storage format (O1).

In this phase (research row I, section 11.2 items 1 to 4):
- The label interoperability table `labelInterop` (feature `label-interop@1`) in the signed policy (45.1-01).
- The signed label binding (4778-style) on release copies and exports (45.1-01, attached in 45.1-04).
- Base TDF manifests from the public OpenTDF specification, with key access to a partner's key and our receiving key in OpenBao (45.1-02).
- The exchange adapter at the release boundary: export and import (45.1-03).

Not in this phase (Phase 45.2, trigger: your organization receives ACP-240 Ed A with its ZTDF specification and ADatP-4774 and 4778):
- The ADatP-4774 XML serializer and parser, the ADatP-4778 binding object and its profiles, ZTDF assertions and label placement, key access to a partner key access server, XML canonicalization, and conformance against allied sample objects.

Also not here: a cross-domain guard between networks of different classification (pitfall 14), federation transport (L5), and export-control person decisions (Phase 45).

## Implementation Decisions

- **D-01 (two parts, two phases):** Part 1 is this phase. Part 2 is the stub Phase 45.2, planned when its trigger happens, so this phase can be verified and closed on its own. The trigger is the texts, not a date.
- **D-02 (the label table):** `labelInterop` (feature `label-interop@1`) is a section of the signed policy. For each scale preset it names an abstract policy identifier; for each level a classification value; for compartments a restrictive category; for releasability the permissive "Releasable To" category; with a mapping version. The table is abstract (identifier, value, category type), so only the XML serializer waits for ADatP-4774. Your organization's security officer confirms the values (Phase 70 research flag 5 already asks for this); the shipped default has no NATO values and refuses every export until they are set. A label with no mapping is refused for export. Mapping in and back out gives the same label (property test).
- **D-03 (the signed label binding, revised 2026-11-24 after the coordinator's cross-branch check B6):** `LabelBinding { label, mapping, contentHash (SHA-256), signer, time }`, signed with `eddsa-jcs-2022` through Phase 71's `CredentialSigner` (71-01, 71-03). It never carries a person DID or a person key, and is never signed with a person key, because observers must see no staff identity (33.1 D-27, D-35). `signer` is one of two forms only: `session:<writer>`, the opaque per-sign-in writer id of the certified staff session key that already signs `release.copy` in the observer stream (33.1 D-35), or `org-release:<site>`, your organization's release key for exports to partners: a non-exportable ed25519 Transit key `release-<site>` that signs only for a step-up token of the release authorities the site file's `releaseAuthorities` names (the custody pattern of 71 D-14). The releasing person's identity stays in your organization's own log (the person-key act, and `exchange.export` for exports). A receiver checks that the label belongs to the content and that neither changed. `bindLabel(signer, binding)` and `verifyLabelBinding(value, content)` live in `src/domain/exchange/labelBinding.ts`. Once ADatP-4778 is read (45.2), the same facts are written as a 4778 binding object.
- **D-04 (the adapter interface):** `ExchangeAdapter { id, formats, canExport(label, agreement), export(copy, recipient), import(object) }` in `src/domain/exchange/adapter.ts`. Part 1 ships one adapter, `tdf`.
- **D-05 (export is a release):** An export goes through the same `mayRelease` (71-07) against the partner's tokens from an `agreements@1` record (71-06) whose ceiling covers the label (the agreement stands for the partner's people only when your organization has vetted the partner, D-12), requires the releasing person's `ReleaseAuthorityCredential`, is a person act signed after a step-up, and is recorded in the project's base cell as `exchange.export { receipt, agreement, binding, format }` with the receipt as a sealed blob (feature `exchange@1` through `roster.require`). Content carrying `CGP`, `ITAR` or `EAR` is refused until Phase 45 ships its per-regime decision (fail closed). Part 1 exports released versions of staff projects (the 33.1 release act), which is where a person already decided.
- **D-06 (import):** Import verifies the label binding and the TDF policy binding, maps the label in through the table, holds the object in quarantine until a person accepts it, and raises the session mark to the join of the labels when it is opened (Phase 70). An object whose label does not map, or whose binding fails, is refused with a plain reason.
- **D-07 (base TDF):** From the public OpenTDF specification 4.3.0 (BSD-3-Clause-Clear): the manifest schema files are copied as test data after review and never run. Payload AES-256-GCM; key access to the partner's public key, `ec:secp256r1` (ECDH with `node:crypto`) or `rsa:2048` (RSA-OAEP); the policy binding as the specification says. An object addressed to us is opened with our own receiving key, non-exportable in OpenBao Transit. Transit has no ECDH, so the receiving key is RSA (`exchange-receive-<site>`, RSA-3072, decrypt only), usable only through a step-up token of the exchange officers the site file's `exchangeOfficers` names (the same custody-by-identity pattern as 71 D-14); whether partners accept RSA-OAEP with SHA-256 or need SHA-1 is checked in 45.1-02's research task.
- **D-08 (not accreditation):** The adapter moves objects between accredited systems at the same classification. It is not a cross-domain guard. SECURITY.md says so.
- **D-09 (order):** Depends on Phase 71 (core): 71-03 (the profile signer) for 45.1-01, 71-06 (`agreements@1`, and `features.ts` and `policyDocument.ts` in their merged state) for 45.1-01, 71-07 (`mayRelease`) for 45.1-03; and on 33.1-21 (the release act) for 45.1-04. Phase 45 is needed only for export-controlled content, which is refused until then. L5 is not needed for file exchange. It runs beside 33.1's later waves on its own branches (`feat/phase-45.1-NN-<slug>` from `feat/phase-45.1-exchange`).
- **D-10 (no new dependency):** `node:crypto` and existing code. No XML library in part 1. Plain words; "your organization"; never log content, keys or labels' category values.
- **D-11 (owner-directed 2026-11-25 at the 45.1-03 checkpoint: liberal in what we accept, strict in what we keep):** Partners whose systems are not like ours must still be able to send us data, so arrivals are not limited to ZTDF; each is converted on arrival to what we keep where conversion is possible. The record shapes, settled by this answer (permanent once a shared log holds one):
  - `exchange.export { receipt, agreement, binding, format }` as proposed; `format` names what we sent (`tdf` now; `ztdf-profile/1` later, 45.2).
  - `exchange.import { receipt, label, state (quarantined | accepted | rejected), arrived, stored, conversion? }`. `arrived` names the format that came in, from an open, extensible id set (`tdf`, `ztdf-profile/1`, `bdo-4778`, `marked-file` for a plain file with a recognisable marking, `unlabelled`, and later ids). `stored` is a list naming what we kept: our own protected cell always (`['cell']`), plus a ZTDF-compatible form where conversion is possible (`['cell', 'ztdf-profile/1']`, Phase 45.2). `conversion` is optional: it records the converter id and version, and what was lost or assumed (for example "label inferred from banner marking", "no binding signature"), and is absent when an arrival is kept as received (a `ztdf-profile/1` arrival). Amended 2026-11-25 by the coordinator for the 45.2 inbound adapters.
  - Every arrival goes to quarantine first. Converting never raises trust: a converted object is no more trusted than its source proves, and anything without a verifiable binding stays quarantined until a person with the authority labels or accepts it.
  - Version safety: a record is readable only when its `arrived` id is one the client knows and its `stored` list holds `'cell'`. Otherwise the client shows "received in a format this version can't read; ask your administrator" and does nothing with the content. A stored form the client does not know beside `'cell'` is ignored for reading and shown as "Also kept in a form this version can't read". `KNOWN_ARRIVAL_FORMATS` and `KNOWN_STORED_FORMS` in `src/domain/exchange/adapter.ts` are the one authoritative id sets; 45.2 imports them. So the open set cannot make an older client misread a newer format, and a new arrival format needs no new feature version; `exchange@1` covers the family. This settles hand-off H-3 for imports; a new export `format` value is read the same way (shown, never interpreted).
  - Part 1 builds the `tdf` arrival only. Inbound adapters for other formats are planned in Phase 45.2 (45.2 hand-off H-13).
- **D-12 (owner decision 2026-11-25: an agreement stands for a partner's people only when your organization has vetted the partner):** Owner, verbatim: "agreement stands (for nations and orgs we trust and want to allow into the system - it presupposes that we've already vetted their clearance process and have marked them as a trusted partner - if they aren't, that vetting needs to occur first or they need to provide details)."
  - The record: an `agreements@1` item (71-06) gains an optional `clearanceProcessVetted { by, on, basis }`: who in your organization vetted the partner's clearance process (as people read it), when (a timestamp), and on what grounds (text). Only your organization's signed layer can set it: `agreements.items` is already organization-only in `layers.ts`, so a project or personal layer can neither add nor change it, and no layer rule changes. Absent, malformed or dated after now means not vetted (fail closed); nothing ships marking a partner vetted. `agreements@1` is in no release yet (v0.4.38 has none), so an optional field keeps the id's meaning (`features.ts`: append-only once released); if a release carries `agreements@1` before this lands, stop and ask the coordinator (a new id, or a section of its own).
  - The rule, in `canExport` (45.1-03 Task 3): the agreement's ceiling, compartments and tokens bound every export, vetted or not (`mayRelease` with `factsFromAgreement`, D-05). For a vetted partner the agreement then stands for the partner's people, and no per-person credential is asked (as 45.1-03 Task 2 built it for every partner). For a partner that is not vetted, the export also needs the recipient's own verified credentials, given to `canExport` as a recipient `{ did, facts, policy }`: the facts' subject must be that DID (bound in the domain, so 45.2's NATO-format exports and every other caller inherit it), their nationality or organization token is one of the agreement's tokens, and `mayRelease` passes for them with the credentials section of the policy in force, which the type requires on that path and `canExport` also checks at run time, so `requiredBySensitivity` always applies and a missing section refuses (independent check, 2026-11-25). Without them it is refused with "This partner's clearance process hasn't been vetted. Vet it first, or the recipient must provide their clearance details."
  - "They need to provide details": the partner gives the recipient's clearance details to your organization, whose issuers issue the recipient's credentials through the existing chain (71 D-07; a partner's own issuer is not trusted here). The release authority adds them as `.near-vc` files, checked in the backend in a separate, throwaway wallet whose holder is the recipient's DID, never the releaser's own wallet (45.1-04 Task 2). The sealed export receipt records the basis: `vetted-partner`, or `recipient-credentials` with the recipient's credential hashes (45.1-03 Task 4).
  - Scope: only exports rely on an agreement this way. Outside observers already present their own credentials at every release (33.1 D-30, D-37) and imports grant nothing, so neither changes. 45.2's NATO-format exports go through the same `canExport` (45.2 D-14), so the rule covers them; 45.2 hand-off H-6 ("`agreements@1` stays as it is") is superseded for this one optional field. The 71-06 file and its test are edited as a 45.1 task; neither is a gate path (`GATE_PATHS` in `scripts/lib/workflow.mjs`). 45.1-05's SECURITY.md section (a gate path already) states the rule. No gap: nothing is deferred.
