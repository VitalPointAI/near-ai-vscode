## The NEAR AI request (draft, as in 48.4-NEAR-REQUEST.md)

> **Subject:** NEAR AI Cloud private inference: locations, operator, subprocessors and retention, with our hosting request
>
> Hello,
>
> We build Sterling Sovereign AI (SovAI), which uses NEAR AI Cloud's private inference as one of its AI providers. The organizations that use SovAI include Canadian public sector and defence organizations, and they judge every outside service against Canada's published criteria. Among them are two controls of the Canadian Centre for Cyber Security's ITSP.10.033: SA-9(8), where an external service processes and stores data, and SA-400(6), how data in use is protected and where.
>
> SovAI shows each organization a sovereignty card judged against those criteria. Today every NEAR AI row on it reads "Unknown", because we cannot find these facts published. We would like to replace "Unknown" with what you state.
>
> For NEAR AI Cloud's private inference, could you tell us:
>
> 1. **Locations.** The countries of every data centre that runs private inference today, and whether a Canadian region is planned (and when). This is also question N6 of our earlier list.
> 2. **Operator.** The legal entity that operates the service, the country it is incorporated in, and the law it operates under.
> 3. **Subprocessors.** Every subprocessor, including hosting and cloud providers and the owners or operators of machines on the GPU marketplace that may run a request. Can a customer keep its requests off marketplace machines, or within named countries?
> 4. **Retention.** Whether prompts, files or outputs are stored at any point (logs, caches and abuse monitoring included); if so, where, for how long, and who can read them.
> 5. **Contract.** Whether these answers can be stated in a contract or data processing agreement, with notice before any of them changes.
>
> **With our earlier hosting request.** We repeat our request to host, in private inference:
>
> - NVIDIA Nemotron 3 (Nano, Super and Ultra), and a Nemotron vision model if NVIDIA ships one;
> - Cohere Command A+, the only Canadian-origin model family, which matters most to the organizations we serve;
> - OpenAI gpt-oss-120b (and gpt-oss-20b);
> - Western embedding and reranking models (NVIDIA or IBM Granite).
>
> Our earlier questions N1 to N8 still stand: hosting of the models above (N1, N2, N2a, N7); provenance fields in `/v1/models` (developer, origin, base model, licence, artifact hash, quantization) (N3); notice before a model's weights, quantization, hosting or id change, and a stable id per artifact (N4); the hash of the loaded model weights in the attestation report (N5); where the private hardware is and whether a Canadian region is possible (N6); and how model licences pass through to customers (N8).
>
> **Why it matters.** Attestation proves which code and which hardware ran a request. It does not show the country or the operator. Without stated locations, operator, subprocessors and retention, we cannot show the organizations we serve that work sent to NEAR AI stays in Canada or under Canadian law, and our card tells them so.
>
> Thank you. We would be glad to talk it through.
>
> [Name]
> [Title]
> Sterling Computers Canada, ULC
