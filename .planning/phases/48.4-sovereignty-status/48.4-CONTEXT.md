- **D-20: Exposure.** The reachable AI services are the policy's endpoints plus, in the workbench, the site's model servers, each reachable only if the live egress fact allows its destination (in-cluster destinations always). With no egress fact, every listed service counts as reachable, so a missing fact can never improve a row. On the desktop every listed endpoint is reachable. A policy change reaches the lens at the next refresh (D-27), so adding an endpoint with region US turns "Where AI runs" to Not met within one re-judge without a reload.
- **D-21: Declared is not verified.** A fact from the site declaration can reach Met, and its detail always reads "Declared by {name} on {date}, signed with a key your site trusts". Facts from the collector or the release read "Verified". Attestation proves code and weights, never location or operator.
- **D-22: Cross-checks.** A declaration that names N nodes while the collector counts M ≠ N makes "Where the hardware comes from" Not verified with the reason. An egress destination the declaration's `outsideDestinations` does not map makes every row that reads `reach.ts` read it as unmapped reach (D-42). A declaration past 180 days is Stale; past 90 days its freshness reads Ageing.

### The evidence invariant (revision of 2026-11-25)
- **D-41: Met only from positive evidence.** A sovereignty row on the card, and a control the sovereignty rules judge in the Compliance view, reads Met only when every input it depends on is positively evidenced: verified by signature or collected by the site collector, or declared in a verified site declaration within its freshness, and cross-checked against a live fact wherever one exists (D-22). An input that is missing, unrestrictive (an egress fact allowing `0.0.0.0/0`, or any destination the declaration does not map), or self-reported only (the gate's report, D-27) caps the row or control below Met, with the stated reason. Self-reported inputs may lower a row, never raise it. The lens (48.4-05), the backend (48.4-09) and the evidence rules (48.4-15) apply this one rule; each has a test that removes each input in turn and sees Met go.
- **D-42: One predicate for Canadian processing.** `src/domain/sovereignty/reach.ts` (no imports, erasable syntax) maps the verified egress destinations through the declaration's `outsideDestinations` to register entries (the `service:` entries of `compliance/resources.json` with their location and `supplierJurisdiction`, and the exchanges of `compliance/hosted/exchanges.json` with `location` and `operatorJurisdiction`), and gives `canadianProcessing` { met, restrictive, reasons }: met only when a current verified egress fact exists, every outside destination is mapped to a place in Canada, the site's model servers are at a facility declared in Canada, and no reachable endpoint in the gate's report (when present) has a region other than `CA`. The lens's "Where AI runs" and "Where your data is stored" require it; the backend turns it into the derived observation `sovereignty-reach` (its time the older of the egress fact and the declaration, its verification the worst of theirs), and SA-9(8)'s statement needs both the declaration rule and the reach rule (48.4-15). So the Compliance view never shows SA-9(8) Met while the card's rows that depend on the same facts are below Met. The destination-to-place mapping (each outside CIDR to a register entry) comes only from the declaration: software cannot check that an address belongs to the named service, so every detail that relies on it, in "Where AI runs", "Where your data is stored" and SA-9(8), labels it "Declared by {name} on {date}" (D-21); the caveat is recorded on G528.
- **D-43: Rows derived from your organization's rules.** "Where AI runs", "Canada-only handling rule", "Where models come from", "Which laws apply to outside services" and the cloud-model part of "Who can switch it off" read Met only when the current verified egress fact restricts outside reach to destinations, mapped by `reach.ts`, that meet the row's criterion. On the desktop, which has no egress fact, they never read Met.
- **D-44: Declarations are monotonic and bound to their site, with a site-level mark sessions cannot write.** A declaration whose `site` differs from the trust document's `site` is Did not verify (`other-site`). The high-water mark lives at the site, signed with your site key: `sterling-sovereignty-mark/1` { `site`, `declaredAt`, `sha256` } is kept in the trust ConfigMap `sterling-evidence-trust` as `sovereignty-mark.json` with its `sovereignty-mark.json.sigstore.json`, a cosign key-mode signature verified in every session against the configured site key exactly as the trust document is (48.2 D-17, D-19). Only the install identity can write that ConfigMap (48.2-17), so neither a session, a person nor the publish identity can lower it. Every session, a fresh one with no state included, refuses a declaration whose `declaredAt` is older than the mark, or equal with a different SHA-256, as Did not verify (`rollback`). With no mark, or a mark that does not verify or names another site, the declaration is Not verified ("no site mark"), so nothing that depends on it can be Met (D-41). Order of a new declaration: the operator publishes it (`site-evidence.mjs publish`, which refuses one older than the current mark or for another site), then the site key holder raises the mark with `evidence-trust.mjs mark`, which signs through the site key's PKCS#11 path (48.1-17), patches only the two mark keys as the install identity, and refuses to lower the mark. The session also keeps the last accepted `declaredAt` and hash in the workbench user's state folder as a second, local check (it catches a rollback between a publish and the next mark). The mark is its own signed file rather than a new field of `sterling-evidence-trust/1`, so a session on an earlier release, which reads only the files it knows, keeps verifying the trust document unchanged (version safety, 32.1 D-60).
