## Phase Boundary

People in assignment modes (Defence Team in the Defence edition; Military, Public service and Capital projects elsewhere) are posted to positions that outlive them, by someone with the authority to do so. They cannot decline: they report for duty, or report unable with a reason the tasker decides on. A handover passes a position's authority and its keys from one holder to the next at one roster entry, so a position is never empty by handover and never held by two. Everything runs on the Phase 33 shared roster and hub, signs through the Phase 46 key service, and shows in the native Sterling Staff Room and work-area page.

In this phase:
- **Positions and postings as roster kinds** (DESIGN 3.1 to 3.8): position create, update and disestablish; delegation of posting authority under one `mayDelegate` rule; orders, device proof, report for duty, report unable, the tasker's decisions, overdue, acting, temporary, return, relief, vacancy and cancellation; staged key changes that take effect at the report. (POST-02 to POST-07)
- **The `positions@1` feature** on Phase 33's `roster.require`, so an older client or hub stops instead of accepting a relieved holder's writes. (POST-01 is Phase 33's; this phase registers its feature.)
- **The shared project's signed mode and settings** (`project.genesis` mode, `project.mode`), which Phase 41 left to this phase (41 D-22, D-40).
- **Report-unable reasons and order references** in the personnel key group, sealed to the tasker and their authority chain. (POST-08)
- **Migration from seats**, silent positions in invitation mode, one record for the lead, and team files that name positions. (POST-08)
- **How a person serves** (Regular Force, Reserve Force, public servant, contractor) on the member record, and the few words that follow it in the Defence Team mode, which 32.1 D-51 and hosting design O1b hand to this phase.
- **Native Sterling screens:** posting cards and tasker actions in the Staff Room, Post a person in the Share with people section for assignment modes, the posting file opened by a Theia open handler, and a position workspace with a switcher for people who hold two positions.
- **The close-out** on the built Sterling image: two browsers, the blocking authorization checks, SECURITY.md, the full suites once.

Not in this phase:
- Issued direction, the planning cycle and a Staff Center view of it (Codex's staff-teams design 2.11 and the wider part of 2.12): Deferred Ideas.
- Manning documents and directory groups (DESIGN 3.11), credentials and verified DIDs (Phase 71).
- The roster clearance and the shared mode as inputs of chat routing in the gate (D-24).
- Portfolio supply from positions (Phase 38), capital appointment screens (Phase 42), offline transports (Phase 34), shared agent work (Phase 35).
- Any VS Code webview, panel, walkthrough or command for positions (D-07).

## Implementation Decisions

### Codex's decisions (2026-11-23), reviewed 2026-11-24

- **D-01 (kept, refined by D-16):** A position is an organization-owned roster object that outlives its holder. One stable person can hold several positions, each through its own posting. The person is the verified roster member; a DID is resolved only when Phase 71 provides the binding. Refined: in a hosted session the member is found from the session's person key, never from an email, a sign-in name or a claimed DID (D-16).
- **D-02 (kept, refined by D-09, D-17, D-18):** Only an authorized tasker can post someone. Signed delegation, scope, effective dates and the assignment-mode flow are enforced. Proving the device delivers keys and is not consent. The assignee reports for duty or reports unable, and the tasker decides any exception. Refined: every posting act is signed by the person key after a fresh passkey step-up (D-09); in a hosted session the "device" is the person's key in your organization's key store (D-17); the posting file opens in Sterling's Staff Room (D-18).
- **D-03 (kept, changed by D-13):** A handover changes effective authority and access keys at the same roster entry. Acting and temporary postings keep one effective holder. Historical signer, position and decision stay attributed after handover. Changed: Codex's "restage automatically once per applicable head" holds only where the preparer can sign without a new prompt, and only the lead or a tasker who already holds a key group prepares its next key (D-13).
- **D-04 (kept, changed by D-10, D-20):** A person works in one active position at a time; switching cannot carry another posting's authority; protected actions recheck the current posting; expired or relieved access is removed; separation of duties holds for dual-hatted people. Changed: the acting position is cited inside the sealed entry body, with no planning-cycle id (D-10); the workspace filter is scoping of a person's own positions, not the confidentiality boundary between people (D-20).
- **D-05 (kept):** Presence reuses the hub's authorized presence (Phase 33-12). Presence is session activity: fresh, expired and disconnected are told apart, and none of them grants or keeps authority. Invitation-mode projects keep their behaviour. A client or hub without `positions@1` refuses the project through POST-01, with the D-67 sentence (D-22).
- **Codex WIP on Plan 05 (kept):** relief uses Phase 33's causal rules against the acting position's cutoff. Decisions causally before the relief stand. Acts causally after it, and acts signed after the cutoff that cite the withdrawn position, are refused. An uncertain concurrent act waits until a current authorized holder confirms it. A backdated time alone never shows that an outgoing holder acted before relief. D-12 extends this to date-ended postings.

**Signed assignment boundary**
- **D-09 (who signs what):** Every new roster kind (`position.create`, `position.update`, `position.disestablish`, `authority.delegate`, `authority.revoke`, `posting.order`, `posting.enrol`, `posting.report`, `posting.decide`, `posting.return`, `posting.end`, `posting.cancel`, `cell.rekey.staged`), `posting.unable` and `project.mode` joins Phase 33's one `PERSON_KEY_KINDS` list (33-21). In a hosted session they are signed by the person key through `KeyService.sign` after a fresh passkey step-up (46-07 D-21); on a single machine by the device key. A session key never signs them: the fold and the hub refuse with person-key-required, and the engine's one append path (`appendOwn`) refuses to sign a person-key kind with anything but the person signer (33.1-17). One invariant governs confirmation: a step-up token signs exactly one action, the one whose request id the step-up was started for and whose summary the person saw (33.1-17). The backend creates every action id, request id and nonce for a service call from the stored payload and never takes them from a browser message. The step-up address carries the action's backend nonce through the identity provider round trip. Only the token seen on the return request with that nonce is bound to that action; a token that returns with no matching pending action signs nothing, and nothing binds a token merely because it arrived next. Before the round trip the backend shows a summary page made from the stored payload. A session holds at most one pending step-up action (a new one cancels the earlier). A pending action expires within the step-up window (`STERLING_STEPUP_MAX_AGE_SECONDS`), and sooner if never confirmed. `resumeAction` completes it atomically before signing. The registry is in memory only, so a restart drops it. One action's entries (an order and its staged key changes, or one report) are signed under that one confirmation; any other action, even inside the window, asks again. In a hosted project every person-key kind outside Phase 33's own list must carry an action id, so a 33.1 act that forgets one is refused even inside an open window; only Phase 33's own kinds keep signing without one. Agents never order, enrol, report, decide or delegate: the fold and the hub refuse an agent or hub actor on every one of these kinds (DESIGN 3.4).
- **D-10 (the acting position in the entry):** A protected act names the position it is made as, inside the sealed body: `actor: { kind: 'person', position: '<position id>' }`, accepted only behind `positions@1` (an older client stops at the require, so its closed actor check never sees it). Acts that cite a position: every position, authority and posting kind (the tasker's position, so the authority chain is checkable), and every versions-family act (propose, verdict, submit, withdraw, decide, release, supersede). `doc.update` and `doc.compact` cite none: who may type is decided by the key group's current epoch, which a relief changes. No header change and no planning-cycle id. Enforcement in three places: the person's own session backend checks before it signs; the fold checks the cited position against the signer's member and holding interval at the cited roster, for device and session-key writers alike; the hub never reads `actor.position` outside the base cell and checks only what it can read: every roster-family kind in the base cell (delegate scope, self-report, enrolment), the epoch currency of every append after a relief, and the member-level date-end check of D-12.
- **D-11 (the shared project's signed mode and settings):** A shared project's mode is signed data, not a local file. `project.mode { mode, settings, policy, reason }` sets or changes it; `project.genesis` is unchanged (D-26 writes the first `project.mode` right after genesis instead, so no genesis field is added). It is a roster-family kind despite its name (as `hub.admit` and `lead.handover` are): it is written by the lead position's holder with the person key, it is linear in the roster chain, so "refused while a posting or invitation is pending" is decided in one order on every replica, and the hub's roster cache folds it in the base cell. Its fields: `mode` within your organization's `allowedModes`; `settings` the Phase 41 behaviour settings the fold needs (`maxDelegationDepth`, `overdueDays`, `enrolAtReport`, `acting`, `temporary`, `dualHatting`, `guests`), each no looser than the mode allows (`effectiveBehaviour`); `policy` the hash of the organization policy it was written under. It is refused while a posting or an invitation is pending (DESIGN 5.7). The fold uses only these signed values, so every replica and the hub fold the same numbers; a fold that read each member's own policy would make replicas disagree. Your organization's rules reach the workbench through one hidden extension command, `nearAi.organizationPeople` (the 32.1 D-46 bridge pattern), which returns the effective `people.organization` section and the personal clearances as plain data: the lead's backend writes `project.mode` from it, and a person whose own rules are tighter than the signed settings cannot sign an act those rules forbid. Their backend refuses with "Your organization's rules changed. Ask the {lead} to update how this {folder} works.", and the lead's Share with people section offers one action to write a new `project.mode`. The fold enforces `mayDecline` from the signed mode: in an assignment or appointment mode a `member.invite` folds only for a guest when the signed settings allow guests, and is refused otherwise ("People join this shared work by {join word}. Ask the {tasker} to post them."). A project with no signed mode behaves as Corporate (portfolio D12), as today.
- **D-12 (time):** The roster fold keeps its injected `now` only for what it already uses it for (the future hold) and for display states. Authority comes from roster order and signed entry times. Start dates: a report counts on or after its start, by its signed time, held by the existing future hold. Relief by a roster entry: the Codex WIP rule above. Date-ended postings (temporary end, acting end) write no roster entry, so neither a relief nor a rekey marks them; for writes the hub uses a per-member check (33.1-14). For each append the hub takes the writer's member (a device's member, or a session key's certificate member) and the key group of the stream, from the header and the base-cell roster, and refuses with posting-ended when every basis that member has for that key group is a posting whose signed end plus the 10-minute skew is before the hub's receive time; a grant of the member's own, or another posting still in effect, keeps the append. This needs no body, so it covers `doc.update`, which cites no position (D-10). Reading is cut separately: the end-date key change is staged at order time like a handover's (33.1-05, 33.1-07), and writers switch to it at the end. Offline, an act by that holder that the newest hub checkpoint does not show as received before the end waits until a current authorized holder confirms it (the lead when the position is vacant). Overdue is a display state from the signed start, the signed `overdueDays` and the injected clock. New domain files never call `Date.now()` or `new Date()`.
- **D-13 (who prepares a key change):** A `cell.rekey.staged` for a key group is written by the lead, or by a tasker who already holds that key group at the cited roster, and never by the outgoing holder. A delegate who does not hold the key group gets the order signed and the stage waits for the lead: the lead's Staff Room shows "Prepare the key change for this handover" with one action, and the order card names who it waits for. When a stage goes stale (DESIGN 3.6), the preparer's backend restages it automatically only when it can sign without a new prompt for this same action: on a single machine with the device key; in a hosted session only inside the person action that made it stale (for example the lead's own removal of a member), whose step-up the person signer accepts for that action's id (D-09). A step-up taken for any other action, even one still inside its window, never signs a restage. Otherwise the preparer sees "The key change for this handover must be prepared again." with Prepare again, which asks for the passkey. The incoming person's Report for duty refuses with "The key change for this handover must be prepared again before you can {report}." Nothing waits silently: each waiting state names who acts.
- **D-14 (one record for the lead):** Once `positions@1` is on, the lead and the deputy are the holders of the lead and deputy positions, and `leadAt` and `deputyAt` read them. In invitation mode `lead.handover` and `deputy.name` keep working and move the silent position's holder in the same entry, so nothing on screen changes. In assignment and appointment modes they are refused ("Post a person to the lead position instead."), and the lead changes only by posting and handover. Two records never disagree about who leads.
- **D-15 (who may be posted):** An order is refused when the person's roster clearance (on the member record, or on the order for a new person) does not cover the position's required clearance: this is the fold's check, the same on every replica, because no other replica can see the person's personal layer. At Report for duty the person's own backend also checks the meet of that roster clearance and their personal declaration (Phase 41 D-35, through `nearAi.organizationPeople`); when the meet does not cover the position it offers Report unable with "not cleared" instead of reporting. An order is also refused when the person serves in a way the position does not list (`eligibleServes`); or, in Capital projects mode, when the order cites no instrument (DESIGN 5.3). Default `eligibleServes`, owner item 6: a position that decides a product, the lead and deputy positions, and delegated posting authority are open to Regular Force, Reserve Force and public servants; every other position is open to all four ways of serving; the lead may set a position's own list. Refusals: "{name} is not cleared for {position}.", "A contractor cannot hold a position that decides or posts people."

**Identity boundary**
- **D-16 (who "me" is):** The person is the roster member id. In a hosted session the backend reads the public key of the person's Transit key (`person-<entity>`, 46-07) from `KeyService`; "me" is the member whose admitted device is that key at the roster the screen shows, and a session key resolves to its member through `session.certify` (33-21). On a single machine "me" is the member of this device's key. An email, a sign-in name, a display name or a DID never selects a member or a position. Member records gain an optional `did`, written only by the lead or deputy through `member.update`, or by the tasker's order for a new person (delegates stay roster writers for position and posting kinds only, DESIGN 3.2); it carries no authority until Phase 71 verifies it (41 hand-over). A new person named by an order becomes a member only at `posting.enrol`, after the six-word comparison, with the order's name, `serves` and clearance.
- **D-17 (proving it is you, hosted):** In a hosted session the device the posting enrols is the person's key in your organization's key store plus the person's X25519 key from the 33-23 envelope, so later sessions need no new proof. The device request is signed by the person key (after a step-up) and carries a MAC under the posting's one-time secret; the six-word comparison with the tasker stays, because it proves the keys belong to the person the tasker is talking to. Words (owner item 7): hosted, the action is "Confirm it is you" and the line is "This makes sure the keys reach you and no one else. It does not ask whether you accept the {join word}."; on a single machine, DESIGN 3.5's "Prove this computer" and "This makes sure the keys go to your own computer. It does not ask whether you accept the {join word}." An expired secret stops device proof only; the tasker sends a new posting file and the order stays in force. No report rests on the posting file alone: Report for duty and Report unable are both signed by the key enrolled for that posting by `posting.enrol` (the person key after a step-up in a hosted session, the device key on a single machine), and every replica verifies them against that enrolled key in the base roster. A person who has not proven yet and chooses Report unable is taken through Confirm it is you (the six-word comparison) first; the posting secret's MAC only binds the device request, never a report.
- **D-18 (how a posting arrives):** A posting file is its own type, `.near-staff-posting` (purpose `staff-posting`), signed by the tasker over the genesis hash, posting id, your organization's server address and pin and the one-time secret hash. In Sterling a Theia open handler opens it into the person's posting card in the Staff Room: no Decline, the card's actions only. The hub path reuses Phase 33's pending-device exchange with an explicit posting purpose and id at both ends, and lands the same `posting.enrol` as the file. A bad signature or a mismatched file shows the refusal and changes nothing. Set up Sterling (32.1) is not involved.

**Audit boundary**
- **D-19 (the record):** The signed shared log is the one record of who posted whom, under whose authority, when; who reported; who decided as which position; who was relieved and when. `orderedBy` (member and device) and the authority chain are derived from the verified signer and the roster at the cited head, never taken from the entry's own claims. History renders from the roster at each entry's citation, so a relieved holder's decisions keep their person and position. Order references and report-unable reasons live only in the personnel key group, sealed to the ordering tasker and their authority chain; they never appear in the base roster, a log line, a refusal text, presence, an export, the compliance evidence or portfolio supply. `session.certify` (33-21) records session starts; nothing records session ends; no new activity log, monitoring surface or polling loop is added. In a hosted site OpenBao's audit device (Phase 47) records each person-key signature on its own; this phase adds nothing there. The base roster (positions, postings, names, how each person serves, delegations) is readable by every member and by your organization's server, as the roster is today, so nothing private goes into it.
- **D-20 (what the position workspace is):** Each hosted backend serves one person (46 D-03), and a person decrypts only the key groups granted to them; the key groups and the personnel key group are the confidentiality boundary between people. The position workspace narrows what a person's own positions show and which actions each offers, and the active position travels with every protected act (D-10). On relief, expiry or revocation the workspace drops that position's actions and stops showing its new work at once; what the person already received stays readable on their own store, as DESIGN 3.6 says ("You keep what you already received."). Codex's "Protected workspace content clears on relief" reads this way.

**Words**
- **D-21 (Defence Team and how a person serves):** The Defence edition uses the `defence-team` mode (32.1-22). Member records gain an optional `serves`: `regular-force`, `reserve-force`, `public-servant` or `contractor`, set by the tasker's order for a new person and by the lead or deputy through `member.update` for others, visible like the name (D-19). In the Defence Team mode the words for `join`, `joined`, `report`, `acting`, `temporary`, `relieved` and `member` follow the person; every other concept follows the mode. `term(concept, mode, serves?)` returns them from the one mode table. Proposed (status best-guess until the owner confirms, O1b): Regular and Reserve Force take the Military words; a public servant takes the Public service words for those seven concepts ("Assignment", "Assigned", "Report to the position", "Acting appointment", "Temporary assignment", "Assignment ended", "Employee"); a contractor takes the public servant's words with member "Contractor". Codex's criterion 5 wording "switched to Military mode" reads "switched to an assignment mode".
