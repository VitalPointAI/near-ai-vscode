# J5 plans and policy agent team: doctrine research (US, Canadian, NATO)

Research note for the second staff team demo: a J5 (plans and policy) team template for the NEAR AI VS Code extension that can plan at the strategic, operational and tactical levels under US and Canadian doctrine.

- **Researched:** 2026-11-18
- **Mode:** ecosystem and doctrine survey, opinionated
- **Sources:** public, unclassified publications only. Every claim carries a source tag such as [S1 III-11]. Each tag resolves in the Sources section at the end to a URL, the publication title and its date. Where a page or chapter number helps, it follows the tag.
- **How the sources were read:** the Joint Staff and Army Publishing Directorate sites refuse automated downloads, so JP 5-0, JP 3-33, FM 5-0 and FM 3-0 were read in full from Internet Archive copies of the official PDFs (the archive URL is given next to each). AJP-5 Edition B and the Canadian Army OPP handbook were read in full. Other sources were read as web pages. Nothing was run from any downloaded file.
- **Confidence tags:** HIGH (read in the primary publication), MEDIUM (official secondary source, or primary but not the newest edition), LOW (vendor or single secondary source, or inference). Inferences are labelled as such.

## Summary (key points first)

1. **The process is the same seven-step backbone everywhere, with different names and groupings.** US joint (JP 5-0), US Army (FM 5-0 MDMP), NATO (AJP-5 Edition B) and NATO land tactical (APP-28) all run: start, understand the mission, build options, test options, compare, commander decides, write the plan or order. Canada's CF OPP packs the same work into five stages (initiation, orientation, COA development, plan development, plan review) and adds plan review as an explicit stage. One team template can serve all of them by switching a "doctrine profile" that renames steps, products and annex letters. [S1 III-11, S2 1-57, S5 3.1, S6 pp. 14-15, S15] HIGH
2. **"US JPP/MDM" most likely means JPP plus MDMP.** JPP is the joint process (combatant command and JTF); MDMP is the Army process (corps down to battalion). Multi-domain operations (MDO) is not a planning process. It is the Army's operational concept, and it changes what the plan must contain (convergence across domains, windows of opportunity), not the steps. The current FM 3-0 is the March 2025 edition, which superseded the October 2022 edition the owner named. [S3 foreword, 1-9, 3-13; S16; S17] HIGH
3. **Canadian doctrine is in transition, which matters for a Canadian demo.** The CF OPP (CFJP 5.0, April 2008) has been the CAF's joint planning basis since 2002, but the Canadian Army said it would adopt NATO APP-28 for tactical planning by August 2026, the Canadian Forces College intends to teach AJP-5 from the 2026/2027 academic year, and the Canadian Joint Warfare Centre and Strategic Joint Staff plan to align joint doctrine with AJP-5. AJP-5 Edition B was promulgated by NATO on 17 June 2026. The template should offer both "CAF OPP (legacy, still widely taught)" and "NATO AJP-5 / APP-28 (CAF target)" profiles. [S7, S10, S5 letter of promulgation] HIGH
4. **The J5 leads deliberate and future planning; the J35 does future operations (branches); the J33 runs current operations.** At a JTF, J5 future plans look past roughly 96 hours and do sequels, J35 future operations cover roughly 24 to 96 hours and do branches, and J33 current operations work inside 24 hours. At brigade there is no J5: the US Army brigade plans cell is led by the S-3, and battalions have no plans cell at all. The Bastion library swapped J5 and J35; the template must not. [S4 IX-1, II-16, II-20; S2 1-76 to 1-80; internal note 00-bastion-staff-teams.md section 6] HIGH
5. **Parallel COA development is doctrinally allowed but has a known failure mode.** JP 5-0 permits simultaneous COA development by separate groups and warns it can break up the group's synergy and produce COAs that are not distinct. AJP-5 says the planning group "typically" forms teams to produce COAs. The team should run two COA cells in parallel, each given a distinct theme by the lead before starting, then test every COA against the five validity criteria (adequate, feasible, acceptable, distinguishable, complete). [S1 III-35, III-41 to III-42; S5 3.47] HIGH
6. **Red cell and red team are two different jobs.** The red cell belongs to the J2 and plays the enemy in the wargame. The red team is an independent group that challenges the planners' assumptions and logic; it has most effect during mission analysis and COA analysis. Doctrine says do not confuse them; the template needs both. [S1 III-77, J-1 to J-3; S5 UK 2.15] HIGH
7. **The commander decides; the staff and agents only recommend.** FM 5-0 says the responsibility for planning is inherent in command and lists lack of commander involvement and late commander decisions as the first two planning pitfalls. The Canadian handbook says "The OPP belongs to the Commander: the Chief of Staff (COS) and G5 manage it on his behalf." The DND/CAF AI strategy requires that using AI to augment human judgement be justified, documented, accountable, traceable and explainable. Every approval in the workflow (restated mission, intent, COA selection, plan approval) is a human gate. [S2 1-135 to 1-137; S6 p. 12; S24] HIGH
8. **Proposed team: 12 members, the extension's hard limit.** A lead planner (chief of plans and wargame chief controller), a strategy and policy planner (also covers POLAD-style political-military work), an operational design planner, two parallel COA cells, a J2 red cell seat, an independent red team, a J3 future operations seat, a J4 sustainment seat, an assessment planner, a legal adviser seat, and a plans writer for orders production. J2, J3 and J4 seats are "functional representative" members that hand off to the J2, J3 and J4 team templates when those exist. [src/agent/teams.ts line 125; S1 III-1 to III-2; S4 IX-7] HIGH for doctrine, the composition itself is a design recommendation.
9. **Annex letters differ between joint and Army orders, a classic error.** In a joint plan Annex D is Logistics; in an Army order Annex D is Fires and Annex F is Sustainment. The plans writer must take its annex list from the selected doctrine profile. [S1 I-12; S2 Appendix E list] HIGH
10. **Classification is the main product risk.** Everything here is unclassified, but a planning team naturally compiles material, and Canadian guidance warns that "If information is bundled together, the bundle may be more sensitive than its parts." The template should be private by default, refuse classified input, and tell the user that real operational plans belong on accredited systems. [S26; S25] HIGH

Why this composition:

- **Every product in Table 4 has exactly one drafting owner,** which fixes the "duplicate COA developers" and "products with no producer" defects found in the Bastion library (internal note 00-bastion-staff-teams.md section 6, items 3 and 4).
- **Two COA cells, not three,** because doctrine normally compares a small set of distinct COAs and the 12-member cap would otherwise squeeze out legal or assessment. A third COA can be drafted by the design planner when the commander asks for it. (Design choice; doctrine does not fix the number.)
- **POLAD folds into strategy-policy-planner and the wargame director folds into lead-planner.** Both mirror doctrine: the JTF J5 policy division handles national policy compliance [S4 IX-1], and in Canadian practice the G5 is the wargame chief controller under the COS as referee [S6 p. 78]. In US Army practice the COS or XO is the controller [S2 5-148]; the US profile should show the human COS as referee in the wargame step.
- **The red team reports to the human COS, not to the lead planner,** because doctrine describes it as an independent group [S1 III-77]. Its tools exclude writing to plan files, so it can only critique.

## Details

### 1. US joint doctrine (JP 5-0, JP 3-33)

**The joint planning process.** JP 5-0 (1 December 2020) lists seven steps: planning initiation, mission analysis, COA development, COA analysis and wargaming, COA comparison, COA approval, and plan or order development [S1 III-11 to III-63]. These sit inside four planning functions (strategic guidance, concept development, plan development, plan assessment), which can be compressed in a crisis, and planning activities "may be performed sequentially or concurrently" [S1 I-13, I-14 figure]. HIGH.

**Mission analysis outputs.** JP 5-0 lists staff estimates, the mission statement, the commander's refined operational approach (with the JFC's intent statement and updated planning guidance), problem framing, initial force identification, mission success criteria, an initial risk assessment, the mission analysis briefing, initial CCIRs, COA evaluation criteria, the essential, specified and implied tasks, and friendly and threat centers of gravity [S1 III-15 figures III-5 and III-6]. After the mission analysis briefing the commander approves a restated mission, which "becomes the unit mission" [S1 III-29]. The example briefing includes a "Proposed commander's intent" slide, which is the staff input the J5 drafts [S1 figure III-12]. HIGH.

**COA development technique.** JP 5-0 says the first decision is simultaneous or sequential COA development. Simultaneous development can save time, but "the synergy of the JPG may be disrupted by breaking up the team", it needs component and directorate representation in each COA group, and "there is an increased likelihood the COAs will lack distinctiveness"; the time saving "is not an automatic result" [S1 III-35]. Each COA is tested as adequate, feasible, acceptable, distinguishable and complete [S1 III-41 to III-42]. HIGH.

**COA analysis outputs.** Wargamed COAs with graphic and narrative and branches and sequels identified, information on evaluation criteria, initial task organization, critical events and decision points, resource shortfalls, refined CCIRs and event template, initial DST and DSM, refined synchronization matrix, refined staff estimates, and the assessment plan and criteria [S1 III-54 to III-55]. HIGH.

**COA approval and plan development.** The staff briefs the comparison and wargame results and recommends a COA; in some cases the JFC presents two or more valid COAs for approval by higher authority [S1 III-58]. The CONOPS states the commander's intent, the central approach, sequencing and synchronization, and focuses on friendly and enemy COGs [S1 III-62 to III-63]. HIGH.

**Operational design.** JP 5-0's elements of operational design are objectives, military end state, center of gravity, effects, culmination, lines of operation, lines of effort, decisive points, direct and indirect approach, operational reach, arranging operations, anticipation, and forces and functions; "not all of the elements of operational design are required, or appropriate, for all plans" [S1 III-75 figure III-23]. COG analysis breaks the COG into critical capabilities, critical requirements and critical vulnerabilities [S1 IV-23 to IV-25]. The design method runs: understand the strategic direction and guidance, understand the strategic environment, define the problem, develop and then refine operational approaches [S1 IV-15 figure IV-5]. HIGH.

**Campaign planning.** The 2020 edition "expands discussion on global campaigning and campaign planning" [S1 summary of changes; S19]. Campaign plans are a final planning product of contingency-type planning, alongside level 1 to 4 contingency plans [S1 I-14 figure]. HIGH.

**Plan levels and formats.** Level 1 commander's estimate (several COAs, least detail); level 2 base plan (CONOPS, major forces, support concepts, timelines, normally no annexes); level 3 CONPLAN (abbreviated OPLAN with plan summary, base plan and usually annexes A, B, C, D, J, K, S, V and Z; "3T" when a TPFDD is directed); level 4 OPLAN (complete plan with all applicable annexes and a TPFDL and transportation-feasible notional TPFDD) [S1 I-11 to I-12]. Formats for plans and orders are in CJCSM 3130.03, Planning and Execution Formats and Guidance [S1 I-16, I-18]; that manual was not read for this note. HIGH for JP 5-0 text.

**APEX.** JP 3-33 (2018) says "Joint operation planning is accomplished through the Adaptive Planning and Execution (APEX) system" [S4 IX-5]. A vendor editor's note says the 2017 APEX construct was superseded by the "Joint Planning" construct in JP 5-0 (December 2020) [S20], and the 2020 edition indeed frames the work as operational activities and planning functions of the joint planning and execution community [S1 I-13 to I-14]. Treat "APEX" as legacy wording and use "joint planning" plus "plan levels 1 to 4". MEDIUM.

**Plan transition.** JP 5-0 shows the J-5 to J-3 hand-off of an approved level 2, 3 or 4 plan: stand up the JPG, validate facts and assumptions, mission statement and intent, develop the transition brief, then the "Formal transition of JPG lead from J-5 personnel to J-3 personnel" [S1 VII-3 to VII-4 figure VII-1]. HIGH.

**JTF J5 duties (JP 3-33, 2018).** Lead the JTF planning process; develop, revise and coordinate plans; prepare branches and sequels as directed during execution; prepare the J-5 staff estimate; coordinate with higher, lower, adjacent, multinational and interagency planners; determine forces required and coordinate deployment planning; review TPFDD input; develop operational limitations with J3 and SJA; recommend MOEs and MOPs; and "Form and supervise future plans through the JPG" [S4 IX-2 to IX-5]. A notional JTF J5 has policy, plans, strategy, deployment support and the JPG [S4 IX-3 figure IX-1]. Planning horizons: current operations inside 24 hours, future operations 24 to 96 hours, future plans beyond 96 hours and possibly over six months; future operations focus on branches, future plans on sequels [S4 IX-1]. MEDIUM (2018 text; check the newer edition).

### 2. US Army doctrine (FM 5-0, FM 3-0)

**MDMP.** Seven steps: receipt of mission, mission analysis, COA development, COA analysis, COA comparison, COA approval, and orders production, dissemination and transition [S2 1-57]. Collaborative planning is "two or more echelons planning together in real time", and parallel planning is echelons planning the same operation "nearly simultaneously" [S2 1-119 to 1-120]. Time is allocated with the "one-third, two-thirds rule" [S2 1-118, 5-21 to 5-22]. HIGH.

**Army design methodology (ADM).** ADM is "a methodology for applying critical and creative thinking to understand, visualize, and describe problems and approaches to solving them" [S2 1-54]. Its activities are framing the operational environment, framing problems, developing an operational approach, transitioning to detailed planning and reframing [S2 ch. 4 introduction]. Commanders may start MDMP without ADM when time is short [S2 1-56 note]. HIGH.

**Planning cells.** The plans cell (long range) develops the initial OPORD, branches and sequels and oversees deception planning; at division and above it is led by the G-5, "At brigade the plans cell is led by the brigade operations officer (S-3)", and "Battalions are not resourced with a dedicated plans cell" [S2 1-76]. The future operations cell (mid range) bridges plans and current operations and writes FRAGORDs; divisions and higher have one, brigades and battalions do not [S2 1-77 to 1-79]. A planning team has a lead planner (G-5, G-3 or S-3), a representative per warfighting function, experts and liaison officers, and dissolves when done [S2 1-81]. HIGH.

**Wargame roles.** The COS or XO is "the unbiased controller"; the G-5, G-3 or S-3 portrays the friendly scheme and normally provides the recorder; the G-2 or S-2 role-plays the enemy commander "By trying to realistically win for the enemy"; the plans officer determines branches and sequels; the staff complete the DST and DSM for each COA; the SJA advises on LOAC, ROE and the legal aspects of targeting [S2 5-147 to 5-162]. HIGH.

**Orders.** "The five-paragraph format (situation, mission, execution, sustainment, and command and signal) is the standard for issuing army plans and orders" [S2 1-6]. Army annexes run A Task Organization, B Intelligence, C Operations, D Fires, E Protection, F Sustainment, G Engineer, H Signal, I Air and Missile Defense, J Public Affairs, K Civil Affairs Operations, L Information Collection, M Assessment (G-5 or G-3 or S-3), N Space Operations, P Host-Nation Support, Q Knowledge Management, R Reports, S Special Technical Operations, U Inspector General, V Interorganization-Interagency Coordination, W Operational Contract Support, Z Distribution [S2 Appendix E list]. HIGH.

**Multi-domain operations.** FM 3-0 (March 2025) defines MDO as "the combined arms employment of joint and Army capabilities to create and exploit relative advantages to achieve objectives, defeat enemy forces, and consolidate gains on behalf of joint force commanders" [S3 1-9]. Its tenets are agility, convergence, endurance and depth, which commanders "use... to inform and assess courses of action" [S3 3-7]. Convergence "is a top-down driven outcome from the senior Army tactical echelon" and means synchronizing joint and Army capabilities against combinations of decisive points across domains to create windows of opportunity [S3 3-13 to 3-14]. The operational environment is described through five domains (air, land, maritime, space, cyberspace) and three dimensions (physical, informational, human) [S16; S2 introduction]. Planning implication: in MDO the COA must show which echelon converges which capabilities against which decisive points and when, so the sync matrix needs domain rows and window-of-opportunity markers. HIGH for doctrine; the planning implication is an inference.

### 3. Canadian doctrine

**CF OPP stages.** The Canadian Army staff college handbook frames each stage as a question: Stage 1 Initiation, "What is happening?"; Stage 2 Orientation, "Do I understand the problem and the requirements for a solution?" (mission analysis is the core activity); Stage 3 COA Development, "What options are there for solving the problem, and which option is best?" (includes the wargame, comparison and decision brief); Stage 4 Plan Development, "How do I translate the chosen solution into a plan for action?"; Stage 5 Plan Review [S6 pp. 14-15]. Products: a preliminary warning order (initiation); mission analysis brief, commander's planning guidance, chief of staff's planning directive, warning order, staff orientation and points for clarification (orientation); information brief, wargame products, decision brief, CONOPS and updated warning order (COA development); CONOPS approval by higher, a plan wargame, and plans and orders (plan development); progress and periodic reviews (plan review) [S6 TOC pp. 3-5]. HIGH.

**Commander and staff.** "The OPP belongs to the Commander: the Chief of Staff (COS) and G5 manage it on his behalf"; the commander "may abbreviate or modify the OPP" [S6 p. 12]. Stages 1 to 3 need close commander involvement; Stage 4 "relies heavily on staff team work" [S6 p. 15]. HIGH.

**Canadian wargame team.** Control team: COS as referee ("Final Arbiter") and G5 as chief controller; ECOA team led by G2 Plans as enemy force commander; FCOA team led by G5 Plans or G35 as friendly commander who identifies decision points and branches or sequels; supporting staff from all branches; observers such as liaison officers and other government department representatives [S6 pp. 72-79]. HIGH.

**Differences from US JPP.**
- Five stages instead of seven steps; COA analysis, comparison and decision all sit inside Stage 3 [S6 pp. 14-15].
- Plan review is an explicit stage; in JP 5-0, plan assessment is a planning function rather than a numbered step [S6 p. 69; S1 I-14 figure].
- Canada adds an information brief, where the commander keeps, modifies or drops COAs before the wargame [S6 p. 63].
- The chief of staff issues a planning directive after the commander's planning guidance [S6 TOC p. 4].
- At tactical level, CAOPP has no operational design and treats the end state as given; at operational level, the end state is derived from strategic direction [S7].
HIGH.

**Transition to NATO doctrine.** "By August 2026, the Canadian Army will adopt APP-28 as its tactical planning tool, replacing the CAOPP"; CFC intends to teach AJP-5 in 2026/2027; CJWC and SJS plan to align joint operational planning doctrine with AJP-5 [S7]. AJP-5 includes operational design in mission analysis, while APP-28 omits it from its formal process [S7]. HIGH.

**Strategic level (SJS).** The SJS "provides military analysis and advice to the Chief of the Defence Staff" and has Operations, Support, Plans, Strategic initiatives and Coordination divisions; its Plans division develops contingencies and capacities [S11]. The 2020 evaluation says the SJS role includes "to translate the CDS' intent into strategic directives", planning capacity sits mainly in DG Plans, and CJOC took over developing military options so SJS could focus on strategic-level operational planning [S12]. HIGH.

**Operational level (CJOC and JTFs).** CJOC "plans, directs, and integrates all-domain military operations" in Canada, North America and abroad, with component commands for air, maritime, space and cyber [S13]. Regional JTFs appear in domestic operations such as Operation LENTUS, which "follows an established plan of action" and is used only when no other organization can respond; the Minister approves requests "upon the advice of the Chief of the Defence Staff" [S28]. The internal structure and duties of the CJOC J5 and JTF J5 branches are not described in any public source found. The template should model the Canadian operational J5 on the CF OPP or AJP-5 roles above and say so. LOW for CJOC J5 detail (gap).

**Tactical level (Canadian Army).** The Army OPP handbook guides planners at tactical level [S8]. Battle procedure governs unit and sub-unit planning; a November 2025 opinion article proposes an "integrated" 17-step battle procedure with collaborative planning between echelons [S9]. MEDIUM (the article is an opinion piece, not doctrine).

**Smaller headquarters.** An RCAF Journal article argues the CF OPP "has been optimized for a larger headquarters" and proposes, for an air task force, starting with framing, developing one friendly COA, more wargaming and adding red teaming [S14]. This supports a "light" profile for small staffs. MEDIUM.

### 4. NATO alignment (AJP-5 Edition B, COPD, APP-28)

AJP-5 Edition B has seven steps in four stages: planning initiation; mission analysis; COA development; COA analysis; COA validation and comparison; commander's COA decision; plan development, with OPLAN and CONOPS as the output [S5 3.1 figure 3.1]. "Typically, the JOPG forms teams to produce ideas for possible COAs and to develop tentative COAs", and the commander decides which COAs are analysed further [S5 3.47]. Wargaming should test each COA against the "most likely" and "most dangerous" adversary COAs, and "Wargaming results do not validate or invalidate" COAs by themselves [S5 3.58]. The COPD "is the basic procedural document for planning staffs" in NATO and "contains the standard structure and content of OPLANs" [S5 2.25b]. The UK annex maps AJP-5 to COPD phases: Phase 1 situation awareness, Phase 2 operational appreciation, Phase 3a mission analysis, Phase 3b COA development, Phase 4a CONOPS development, Phase 4b OPLAN development, Phase 5 execution, Phase 6 transition [S5 UK Annex F figure F.1]. Red teaming in AJP-5 is a UK national element ("This technique is not described in NATO doctrine"), while the J2 red cell is common [S5 UK 2.15]. HIGH.

APP-28 tactical planning has three phases and seven steps: receipt of mission and mission analysis (understanding), COA development, analysis and comparison (developing COAs), and commander's decision and transition (communication); units up to corps use APP-28, while higher commands use the COPD [S15]. MEDIUM.

### 6. Classification, handling and decision authority

- **Unclassified only.** Canadian levels: Protected A, B, C for injury outside the national interest; Confidential, Secret, Top Secret for injury to the national interest [S25]. Compiled information can be more sensitive than its parts; DND's example is one address at Protected A versus 10,000 addresses at least Protected B [S26]. US controlled unclassified information is governed by DoDI 5200.48, which covers marking and handling of CUI [S29]. NATO publications carry their own handling rule (AJP-5 is to be handled in accordance with C-M(2002)60) [S5 letter para 4].
- **Template rules:** mark the team `privacy: private` (NEAR TEE or local model only, as the extension supports) [src/agent/teams.ts lines 26 to 36]; refuse files that carry classification or caveat markings above unclassified; add an aggregation warning when a project accumulates real unit, location or capability data; use exercise scenarios for demos.
- **Decision authority:** keep every approval human, record who approved what and when, and keep the red team's dissent visible in the decision brief. This matches doctrine [S2 1-135 to 1-137; S6 p. 12] and the DND/CAF AI strategy requirement that AI-augmented decisions be "justified and documented" with "appropriate accountability measures" and traceable, explainable outcomes [S24].
